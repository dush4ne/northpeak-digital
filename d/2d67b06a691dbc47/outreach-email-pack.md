# Freelancer Outreach Email Pack
*by Northpeak Digital*

Twelve plain-text emails for finding clients honestly. Replace [BRACKETS]. Every template follows one rule: **be specific, useful, and short.**

---

## Before you send anything: the rules

- **Say who you are.** Real name, real business, honest subject line.
- **Include a postal address and a way to opt out** in commercial email. In the US this is required by CAN-SPAM; other countries (EU, Canada, UK) have stricter rules. Check yours.
- **Honor opt-outs right away** and never email that address again.
- **Write to people for whom the offer is relevant.** Don't blast lists you bought.
- **One follow-up, maybe two.** Then stop.
- Keep it under 120 words. Nobody reads more from a stranger.

**Required footer:**
> [Your name], [Business name], [Postal address]. If you'd rather not hear from me, reply "unsubscribe" and I won't contact you again.

---

## The formula (use it for any email)
1. **Why them:** one specific, true observation about their business (not flattery).
2. **What you do:** one sentence, in outcome terms.
3. **Proof:** one small example or result (only a real one).
4. **A small ask:** a question or a 15-minute call, not "buy now".

## Cold emails

### 1. Observation-led
Subject: Quick idea for [COMPANY]'s [PAGE/PRODUCT]
Hi [NAME], I noticed [specific, true observation, e.g., the booking page asks for six fields before showing prices]. I help [type of business] [outcome, e.g., simplify booking so more visitors finish it]. For [similar client], that meant [real result]. Worth a 15-minute chat?
[Footer]

### 2. Referral-led
Subject: [MUTUAL CONTACT] suggested I reach out
Hi [NAME], [MUTUAL CONTACT] mentioned you're [working on / hiring for] [thing]. I [what you do, one line]. Happy to share a few ideas if helpful. No obligation.
[Footer]

### 3. Free-sample offer
Subject: A free [mini-audit/draft] for [COMPANY]
Hi [NAME], I put together [a one-page audit / a sample draft] of [specific thing]. Want me to send it over? It's free, and you're under no obligation.
[Footer]

### 4. Problem question
Subject: Is [problem] on your plate?
Hi [NAME], many [role/industry] tell me [problem] eats [time/money]. Is it a priority for you right now? If so, I have a short idea on how to approach it.
[Footer]

### 5. Portfolio drop
Subject: Recent work in [their industry]
Hi [NAME], I recently finished [project] for [client type]: [result]. Since you work in [industry], I thought it might be relevant. Link: [URL]. Open to talking if it sparks anything.
[Footer]

### 6. Collaboration with agencies
Subject: Overflow capacity for [AGENCY]
Hi [NAME], I'm a [specialty] freelancer with openings from [MONTH]. If you ever need overflow help on [type of work], I can work under your brand with quick turnarounds. Happy to do a small paid test project first.
[Footer]

## Follow-ups

### 7. First follow-up (after 4–5 days)
Subject: Re: [original subject]
Hi [NAME], floating this back up in case it got buried. If [outcome] isn't a priority now, no problem. Just say so and I'll close the loop.

### 8. Last follow-up (after another 7 days)
Subject: Closing the loop
Hi [NAME], I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right and won't follow up again. If things change, you know where to find me. All the best with [project].

---

## Replies

### 9. They're interested
Thanks for replying. To make a useful proposal, could you tell me: (1) the main goal, (2) timeline, (3) rough budget range? Or we can cover it in 15 minutes: [calendar link / two time options].

### 10. "Send me your rates"
Rates depend on scope, so I'd rather quote a specific project than give a number out of context. For a quick ballpark, projects like [example] usually run $[range]. If you share a bit about your goal, I'll send an exact proposal.

### 11. "Not now"
Understood, thanks for letting me know. May I check back in [3 months]? If not, just say so and I won't.

### 12. "Unsubscribe" / "Stop"
Done. You're removed and won't hear from me again. Sorry for the interruption.
