## Part 1: Work out your rate (10 minutes)

**Step 1. Income target.** How much do you want to take home per year after tax? [A]

**Step 2. Add costs and tax.** Software, equipment, insurance, accounting, and a tax set-aside (many freelancers reserve 25–35%; check your local rules). Total yearly costs + tax: [B]

**Step 3. Billable hours.** You can't bill 40 hours a week. A realistic estimate:
- Weeks worked per year: 52 minus holidays, vacation, sick days (e.g., 46)
- Billable hours per week: usually 50–65% of your working week (e.g., 22)
- Billable hours per year = weeks × hours = [C]

**Step 4. Minimum hourly rate** = ( [A] + [B] ) ÷ [C]

**Step 5. Sanity check.** Compare against what others in your field charge. If your minimum is far above the market, raise your value (specialize) before raising the number. If it's below, you can likely charge more.

**Project pricing rule of thumb:** estimate hours, multiply by your rate, then add a buffer of 15–25% for revisions and surprises. Quote the project price, not the hours.

In your words: [restate the client's problem and goal in 2–3 sentences, using their language].

### 2. What I'll deliver
- [Deliverable 1, specific and measurable]
- [Deliverable 2]
- [Deliverable 3]

**Not included:** [list anything the client might assume but isn't covered].

| Phase | What happens | Dates |

| Revisions | [Up to 2 rounds included] | [DATE] |
| Delivery | [Final files + handoff] | [DATE] |

### 4. Investment
| Option | What's included | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | [core deliverables] | $[ ] |

## Part 3: Email scripts

**Sending the proposal**
Subject: Proposal for [PROJECT]
Hi [NAME], thanks for the conversation yesterday. My proposal is attached. The short version: [one-sentence outcome]. The recommended option is [Standard]. I'm happy to walk through it on a 15-minute call if useful.

**Following up after 3–4 days**
Subject: Re: Proposal for [PROJECT]
Hi [NAME], checking in on the proposal. Anything I can clarify, or adjust to fit your timeline or budget?

**When they say "your price is too high"**
Thanks for being upfront. Rather than cut the quality, I can offer two routes: (1) a reduced scope that fits [BUDGET], covering [what's included, what's removed], or (2) a phased approach where we start with [phase 1] and decide on the rest later. Which feels closer to what you need?

**When they ask for a discount**
I keep my rates consistent so every client gets the same attention. What I can do is adjust scope or timing to meet your budget. Want me to send a trimmed version?

**Raising rates with an existing client**
Hi [NAME], I enjoy working together. From [DATE], my rate for new work will be $[NEW] (currently $[OLD]). Projects already in progress stay at the current rate. I wanted to give you plenty of notice.

## Part 4: Pricing mistakes to avoid
- Quoting an hourly rate for a project the client sees as one outcome
- No deposit before starting
- No limit on revisions
- Saying yes to a number on the spot (say "I'll send a proposal by tomorrow")
- Never raising prices as your skills grow
